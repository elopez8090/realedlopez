When someone in Peekskill searches "pizza near me" or "electrician Yorktown," Google shows a small map with three businesses before any regular results. Most local customers never scroll past it.

Getting into that map is mostly about three things you control: your **Google Business Profile**, your **reviews**, and your **website**. None of them require a big budget. Here's how to handle each one, in the order I'd do it.

## Step 1: Claim and complete your Google Business Profile

Your Business Profile is the listing that shows up on Google Maps and in the map results. It matters more for local searches than anything on your website.

1. **Claim it.** Search for your business on Google. If you see "Own this business?", click it. If nothing shows up, create a profile at [business.google.com](https://business.google.com/). Google will ask you to verify that you run the business.
2. **Choose the right primary category.** This is the single most important setting. Pick the most specific one that fits, like "Pizza restaurant" instead of "Restaurant." You can add a few secondary categories, but only ones that truly apply.
3. **Set your location correctly.**
   - If customers come to you, such as a shop, salon, or restaurant, show your street address.
   - If you go to customers, such as a contractor, cleaner, or mobile service, set it up as a **service-area business**. You can hide your home address and list the towns you serve, for example Cortlandt Manor, Peekskill, Croton-on-Hudson, and Yorktown Heights.
4. **Fill in everything.** Add your hours, including holiday hours, phone number, website, services or menu, a short description, and attributes like "wheelchair accessible" or "outdoor seating." Profiles with complete details give Google more to match against searches.
5. **Add real photos.** Upload your storefront (if you have one), interior, products, team, and your work. Real photos beat stock images. Add new ones every so often.

**Two mistakes to avoid:**
- **Don't stuff keywords into your business name.** If your sign says "Hudson Plumbing," don't list yourself as "Hudson Plumbing Best Emergency Plumber Westchester." Google's guidelines forbid it, and profiles get suspended for it.
- **Don't use a virtual office or mailbox** as your address. Use your real location, or hide it as a service-area business.

## Step 2: Earn reviews, and answer them

Reviews affect both whether you show up and whether people choose you once they see you.

- **Ask every happy customer.** In your profile, use **Ask for reviews** to get a short link. Send it in a text or email right after a good job, or print it as a QR code by the register.
- **Make it easy.** One link, one sentence: "If we did a good job, a quick Google review really helps a small business like ours."
- **Answer every review**, good and bad, briefly and politely. A calm reply to a bad review often reassures the next customer more than the review itself worries them.
- **Never buy reviews, write your own, or offer discounts in exchange.** All of these break Google's rules and can get reviews removed or your profile penalized.

## Step 3: Make sure your website backs it up

Google checks your website to confirm what your profile says. Your website also needs to convince the people who click through to it.

- **Match your details exactly.** Your business name, address (or service area), and phone number should be the same on your website, your Business Profile, and directories like Yelp, Apple Maps, and your chamber of commerce listing.
- **Say where you work, in plain words.** A line like "Serving Peekskill, Cortlandt Manor, and northern Westchester" in your homepage text and page titles helps Google connect you to local searches.
- **Give your main services their own pages.** A plumber with separate pages for "water heater repair" and "drain cleaning" can show up for both. One page that lists everything usually ranks for neither.
- **Make it fast on phones.** In my study of local business websites, [only 1 of 55 met Google's mobile speed target](/web-design-peekskill/). A slow site loses people who already found you. See [7 signs your website is costing you customers](/blog/signs-your-website-is-costing-you-customers/) for how to check yours.
- **Put the essentials up top.** On phones, your phone number (tap to call), hours, and address should be visible without scrolling.

## Step 4: Keep it active

Google favors profiles that look current.

- **Post an update** on your profile now and then: a new menu, a seasonal special, an event, or a recent project.
- **Answer questions** people ask on your profile.
- **Update hours** before holidays and closures, so nobody shows up to a locked door.
- **Check your profile every month** for edits suggested by the public. Anyone can suggest a change to your hours or address, and you'll want to catch wrong ones.

## What to expect

Showing up in the map is not instant. A complete profile and a steady flow of reviews usually start to make a difference within a few weeks to a few months, depending on how many competitors are near you. In smaller towns with fewer competitors, it can happen faster than in White Plains or Yonkers.
