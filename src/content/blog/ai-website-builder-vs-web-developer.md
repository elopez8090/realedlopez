"AI website builder" is now one of the most searched phrases among business owners, at about **15,000 searches a month** in the U.S. and rising, according to an Ahrefs analysis by Pronto Marketing. The pitch is hard to ignore: answer a few questions and get a finished website in minutes.

I'm a web developer, so you might expect me to say never use one. That's not my answer. For some businesses an AI builder is the right call, and I tell people that. For others it costs more in the long run than it saves. Here's how to tell which one you are.

## What an AI website builder actually does

You type in your business name and a few details. The builder picks a template, writes first-draft text, adds stock or AI-generated images, and gives you a site you can edit. Then you pay a monthly subscription for as long as the site is online.

Typical starting prices in 2026, billed annually:

| Builder | Starts at |
|---|---|
| GoDaddy | $9.99 a month |
| Hostinger | $2.99 a month on a 48-month contract, $11.99 otherwise |
| Wix | $17 a month ($24 if billed monthly); most businesses need Core at $29 or Business at $39 |
| Squarespace | about $19 a month |
| Shopify (online stores) | $29 a month |

Prices are from Website Builder Expert and Expert Market's 2026 reviews. Check the builder's own site before you sign up, because promotional rates usually go up at renewal.

## When an AI builder is the right choice

- **You're just starting out** and want to test an idea before investing.
- **Your budget is under about $1,000** for the year.
- **You need a few simple pages**: what you do, where you are, how to reach you.
- **You enjoy doing it yourself** and have time to write, take photos, and keep it updated.

If that's you, start with a builder. You can always hire someone later.

## Where AI builders fall short

**The content is generic.** The AI writes what any business of your type might say, and it can't know what makes you different from the shop down the street. Reviewers at Expert Market found AI-generated images used by default and stock photos unrelated to the business they described. You'll still need to rewrite the text and replace the photos, and that's most of the real work of a website.

**You're renting, not owning.** Your site lives on the builder's platform. You can't take your design and pages to another host. If prices go up, and Squarespace raised its plan prices in 2026, your choices are to pay or start over.

**Speed depends on what you put on it.** When I tested 55 Northern Westchester business websites on phones this fall, the 15 sites built on hosted builders like Wix, Squarespace, and GoDaddy scored a median of **60** on Google's mobile test. Sites built other ways scored about the same. But **none of the builder sites** loaded fast enough to meet Google's target. Heavy photos, apps, and pop-ups slow any site down, and builders make them easy to add. The [full study is on my Peekskill page](/web-developer-peekskill/).

**Anything custom gets hard.** Builders handle standard pages well. They struggle with a customer login, a booking flow that matches how you actually schedule, a searchable directory, or connecting your website to the other tools you use. That's often where a business gets the most value from its website, and it's the work I describe on my [custom web apps](/custom-web-apps/) page.

**Your time isn't free.** The site appears in minutes, but a good one still takes many hours of writing, photos, and fiddling, and then ongoing updates. For a busy owner, those hours are the real price.

## What each costs over three years

Here's an honest comparison for a typical small business site:

| | AI website builder | Web developer (me) |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront | $0, plus your time | From $2,500 for a starter site |
| Monthly | $29 to $39 for most business plans | Hosting often $0; domain about $10 to $20 a year |
| Optional support | You do it yourself | Care plan $75 to $200 a month |
| **Three-year total** | **About $1,050 to $1,400**, plus apps and your time | **About $2,550** without a care plan |
| Who owns it | The builder hosts it | You own the code, domain, and accounts |

A builder is cheaper in cash. What you get for the difference is a site written and built for your business, faster loading, local search setup done properly, and someone who handles the technical side. Whether that's worth it depends on how much a new customer is worth to you. For most service businesses, one or two extra jobs a year cover the difference. ## A middle path

You don't have to choose forever.

- **Start with a builder, upgrade later.** When you outgrow it, a developer can rebuild your site and redirect your old page addresses so you keep your Google rankings.
- **Keep your builder, fix what's slow.** If your site is fine but slow or hard to use on phones, a speed and mobile fix is far cheaper than a new site. Mine start at $750.
- **Use AI for the first draft, then edit it yourself.** AI is a good starting point for text. Just rewrite it in your own words, with your real details, before you publish.

## Five questions to decide

1. Is your website mainly a digital business card, or does it need to *do* something, like take bookings, collect forms, or show listings?
2. Do you have 20 or more hours to write, photograph, and set it up, plus time every month to keep it current?
3. Do you depend on customers finding you on Google in your town?
4. Would you be stuck if the builder raised prices or shut down a feature you rely on?
5. Is your business different enough that generic text would undersell it?

If you answered "card," "yes," "no," "no," and "no," an AI builder will probably serve you well. If you answered the other way on two or more, it's worth talking to a developer.
